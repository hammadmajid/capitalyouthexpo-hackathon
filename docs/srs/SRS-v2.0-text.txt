PRODUCT VISION
Executive Product
Definition
SRS v2.0
Islamabad & Rawalpindi
MVP Baseline
PRODUCT DEFINITION
Marketplace + Hostel Operations, Not
Generic Property Software
The platform has two equal product pillars: a customer marketplace for verified hostel discovery
and a focused management system for beds, bookings, residents, rent and complaints.
Core customer promise
Verified hostel. Real available seat.
Real price.
Customers must be able to see where the
hostel is, what each room/seat costs,
which seat is available, what the room
looks like, the security deposit, amenities,
menu, ratings and nearby universities
before deciding.
Core owner promise
Owners manage every owned hostel/branch, see who occupies
each seat, accept bookings, track monthly rent, verify proof,
handle complaints and analyze branch performance from one
account.
Focused MVP operations
Rooms and seat/bed availability
Booking requests and reservation control
Residents and active-stay records
Monthly rent and payment-proof verification
Security deposits
Complaints
Mess/menu and optional transport
Reports and platform verification
Explicitly not an inventory system. The
MVP does not manage general stock, food
inventory, furniture stock control or
warehouse-style inventory.
Detailed implementation rules
Primary success measure. The marketplace is successful only when
customers can reliably find genuine available accommodation and
owners keep branch occupancy and rent data current.
No false availability. Availability displayed to customers must
originate from branch operations, not a separately editable marketing
counter.
Owner utility. If owners cannot complete routine booking, check-in
and rent tasks faster than manual registers/WhatsApp, the product has
failed its operational objective.
Marketplace trust. Verification, transparent pricing, photos and
verified-stay reviews are core product functions rather than optional
marketing features.

Security invariant. Branch isolation must be enforced in backend authorization queries,
not by merely hiding branches from the interface.
Public privacy boundary
Marketplace customers may see whether a particular seat is available or occupied, but they shall never see the identity of the
person occupying a seat. Exact resident-to-seat mapping is private to the Owner and authorized Manager of that branch.
Detailed implementation rules
Object-level checks. Every read/write on Branch, Room, Seat,
ResidentStay, Invoice, Payment and Complaint must verify that the
acting management user is authorized for that branch.
Combined owner view. All-Branches dashboards may aggregate
data, but drill-down actions must still bind to a concrete branch before
mutation.
Manager offboarding. Once a Manager is suspended/deactivated,
existing sessions must be revoked or lose management authorization
immediately.
Public seat data. Public seat identifiers may be shown for selection,
but shall not reveal occupant history or previous residents.

Owner account. Owner self-registration should verify mobile/email

apply; the plaintext is not recoverable after creation.
System temp password. When system-generated, the credential
should use cryptographically secure randomness and be shown only
through the intended onboarding channel.
Invite collision. If the invited mobile already belongs to an existing
customer account, the Manager role is attached after successful
invitation verification rather than creating a duplicate user.
